How to Fall Asleep Faster: 10 Evidence-Based Habits

Better Sleep  About 3 minutes to read

Lying awake is frustrating. These ten habits are supported by sleep research and are worth trying for a couple of weeks.

The ten habits

  1. Keep a fixed wake time. It anchors your body clock.
  2. Get morning light. It helps evening sleepiness arrive on time.
  3. Limit caffeine after midday. It can linger for many hours.
  4. Dim lights and screens for the last hour.
  5. Wind down with a consistent routine.
  6. Keep the bedroom cool, dark and quiet.
  7. Take a warm shower one to two hours before bed.
  8. Move during the day. Exercise helps sleep, but avoid hard workouts right before bed.
  9. Avoid alcohol and heavy meals close to bedtime.
  10. Leave the bed if awake after about 20 minutes and return when sleepy.

Calming a busy mind

Write down tomorrow's tasks before bed or try slow breathing. The cognitive shuffle article covers a mental technique.

Do not chase sleep

Trying hard to sleep makes it harder. Aim to rest and let sleep arrive.

When to see a doctor

If difficulty falling asleep happens three or more nights a week for a month, or affects daytime function, see a doctor. CBT for insomnia is very effective.

Frequently asked questions

How long should it take to fall asleep?

About 10 to 20 minutes for most adults.

Do sleep supplements help?

Evidence varies. Ask a doctor first.

Should I check the time?

Avoid it, as clock-watching increases anxiety.

Summary

  • Consistent timing, light and wind-down matter most.
  • Limit caffeine, alcohol and late screens.
  • Get up if awake for 20 minutes.
  • See a doctor if it persists.

Medical disclaimer: This article is for general information only and is not medical advice. If you have persistent sleep problems or excessive daytime sleepiness, speak with a doctor or sleep specialist.
